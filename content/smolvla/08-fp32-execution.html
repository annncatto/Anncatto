Switching all trainable weights and AdamW moments to FP32 on the same 150-demo dataset and 120-demo checkpoint cuts offline flow loss by 52% (precision updates). Does fixing training precision alone complete the real-robot pickup? Deploying this converged FP32 100k checkpoint on the robot (300 s / 5,832 frames) shows the arm lifting and opening the gripper normally during 20–75 s, then entering a 195-second sawtooth oscillation while the gripper remains closed.

保持 150 条示教与同一 120 条起点不变、将全部可训练权重与 AdamW 矩估计改为 FP32 后，离线 flow loss 下降了 52%（见精度更新）。仅修好训练精度就能跑通真机吗？将已收敛的 FP32 100k 部署上真机（300 s / 5,832 帧），前 75 s 仍能正常接近并抬臂开爪，但 95–290 s 持续处于 195 秒锯齿振荡且夹爪保持闭合。

Offline fit vs closed-loop execution: Offline replay scores one open-loop window; live execution compounds chunk handoffs and actuator lag. Policy requests (down to −119.6), Host-accepted targets (clamped at −100), and pre-request feedback are three distinct traces.

离线拟合 vs 真机闭环：离线回放只衡量单窗开环误差；真机闭环还叠加块交接断层与执行滞后。模型请求（最低 −119.6）、Host 接受目标（截断至 −100）与调用前实测反馈是三条独立序列。

During 95–290 s, requests ramp to −10 and drop 60–75 units every ~0.9 s at chunk handoffs (165 of 182 large jumps). The physical arm lags out of phase between −50 and −80 (19.4 MAE; 15.1 even after 0.95 s alignment), while sensor and motor flags remain healthy.
