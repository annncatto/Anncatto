## Corrections retained

- Compliance is the reciprocal of scalar linear stiffness; nonlinear tangent compliance is dx/dF.
- Reflected rotor inertia is N²Jm at the output; backdrivability also depends on friction and transmission geometry.
- Cassie does not place every motor in the pelvis.
- Surface metal foil improves the reported specific energy absorption; this is not an interlaminar shear-strength claim.
- Material indices depend on the geometric variable allowed to change. The thin-tube exercise distinguishes fixed diameter from fixed wall thickness.
- The SIMP density is dimensionless and distinct from material mass density.

### Technical corrections and consolidation

- Cassie has five actuated and two passive joints per leg; do not repeat the claim that all motors are in the pelvis. Proximal placement remains part of system-level mass-layout analysis.
- DLR LWR III retains the manufacturer’s 14 kg mass / 14 kg maximum payload (1.00). Reuse the existing model-specific arm table rather than replace it with the new slide’s 13.5 kg value or mix rated and conditional maximum loads.
- Keep confirmed ASIMO magnesium/H-section data from Honda’s 2001 development report. Do not transfer a material, transmission or mass claim to every member of a robot family. Early Cheetah tendon–bone load sharing and Mini Cheetah module construction are verified independently; modern humanoid brand lists are consolidated into demonstrated integration mechanisms.
- The hydraulic Atlas example is attributed to Raibert’s first-hand engineering interview (2015); it does not describe the electric generation. Robot methods M/D/F/P/A are qualitative categories, not a numerical effectiveness score.
- Recomputed the arm example: 17.658 → 11.772 N·m (33.3% lower); a uniform-rod/point-mass extension gives 0.640 → 0.410 kg·m². Added transmission mass must be budgeted explicitly.
- The fundamentals property table is retained as illustrative, unspecified-grade data. Capacity/self-weight ratios are 5,136 / 13,493 / 16,267 for a 1 m bar at n=1, not ten times these values.
- Use a single bending convention: z and deflection upward, ux=−z w′, strain=−z w″, My=∫zσdA=−EIw″. Cantilever field drawings show magnitudes.
- Timoshenko applicability is quantified by δs/δb; L/h=10 is a heuristic, not a universal cut-off. The ν=.30, ks=5/6 rectangular tip-loaded example gives .78%, 3.12%, 19.5% at slenderness 10, 5, 2.
- Evaluate normal and shear stress at the same material point before von Mises combination. Linear bending stress and parabolic transverse shear maxima occur at different section positions.
- Correct the density-only “buckling” comparison: Euler buckling with geometrically similar variable-area sections gives √E/ρ. The last diagram calculates and normalizes strength, axial stiffness and buckling indices from the stated table.
- Learning-objective covers and lesson transitions are merged into the corresponding exposition; the note’s book-search instruction is excluded from public prose. Bernd Klein’s 10th-edition original is linked to Springer (2013, DOI 10.1007/978-3-658-02272-3).
